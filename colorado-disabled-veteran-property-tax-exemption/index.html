Property Tax Exemption for Disabled Veterans in Colorado: How It Works With a VA Loan

By Jerry Cusick, Mortgage Loan Officer, NMLS #649445

Last reviewed: September 21, 2026

Colorado offers a property tax exemption for certain qualifying disabled veterans and surviving spouses. For a homeowner with a VA loan, the benefit may reduce property taxes and potentially affect the tax portion of mortgage escrow. Qualification for a VA mortgage does not automatically establish eligibility for this state tax benefit.

What the exemption does

The exemption removes a qualifying portion of a residence’s value from the property tax calculation. That is different from receiving the same dollar amount as a tax refund or a direct reduction in the mortgage balance. Actual savings depend on the applicable tax calculation and the property.

For a qualifying primary residence, the exemption covers 50% of the first $200,000 of actual property value. That means up to $100,000 of value is excluded—not a $100,000 tax credit. For example, a qualifying home valued at $175,000 has $87,500 of exempt value. Your assessor calculates the actual tax effect.

Confirm your eligibility before applying

Gather the documents required by the current application instructions and review the specific eligibility category that applies to you. State property tax rules and VA funding-fee exemptions are separate; qualifying for one does not by itself prove eligibility for the other.

The state’s criteria include qualifying active-duty service and an honorable discharge, plus a VA-established 100% permanent service-connected disability or individual unemployability status. The applicant or spouse must generally own the home, and the applicant must occupy it as a primary residence, on January 1 of the application year. Separate applications cover a surviving spouse of a veteran who previously received the exemption and qualifying Gold Star spouses. Check the state instructions for your category and any exceptions.

How to apply

• Use the Colorado Division of Property Taxation application and instructions for your eligibility category.

• File with the county assessor where the property is located. Prepare the current VA award letter and the supporting service, ownership or spouse documents required by your form.

• Submit through the approved channel and keep confirmation of receipt.

• Follow up with the appropriate office about approval and how the exemption appears on the property’s tax record.

Application deadline update: Colorado SB26-046, effective August 12, 2026, aligns the regular application deadline at July 15 and the late deadline at August 15. Some older state and county instructions still show July 1/August 1. As of September 21, 2026, these annual windows have passed. Ask your assessor about the next application year, current forms and appeal rights before filing.

How tax savings may affect a VA loan payment

When property taxes are paid through a mortgage escrow account, a lower tax obligation may change the required escrow payment. The loan servicer’s analysis and timing determine when that change is reflected. An exemption does not change the loan’s principal-and-interest terms, and it should not be described as an immediate guaranteed payment reduction.

Frequently asked questions

Can this be used alongside a VA funding-fee exemption?

They are separate benefits. Some homeowners may qualify for both, but each has its own eligibility requirements.

Does refinancing automatically end the exemption?

Review the current state and county rules for the property, ownership and occupancy. Confirm any reporting obligations before assuming the exemption continues unchanged.

Will my monthly payment drop as soon as I apply?

An application is not an approval. Any escrow change also depends on the approved tax treatment and the servicer’s processing and analysis.
