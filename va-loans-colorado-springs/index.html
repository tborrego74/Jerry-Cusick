VA Loans in Colorado Springs & El Paso County: A Guide for Fort Carson, Peterson and Schriever Families

By Jerry Cusick, Mortgage Loan Officer, NMLS #649445

Last reviewed: September 21, 2026

Colorado Springs and El Paso County are home to military families connected with Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base and the U.S. Air Force Academy. If you are buying with a VA loan while planning a military move, preparing your eligibility documents and understanding the local purchase process can help you make informed decisions.

Start with your timeline and financing

A PCS move can create several overlapping deadlines: reporting dates, housing arrangements, a home search and loan closing. Begin by discussing your plans with a loan officer, including when orders are expected, when you need to move and whether you are selling another property. Avoid assuming that every step can be completed on a fixed schedule.

Obtaining a COE and discussing pre-approval early can identify questions about eligibility, entitlement and required documents before you make an offer. Occupancy and relocation circumstances should be reviewed for your specific situation.

Plan your home search around your installation

The right location depends on more than a base name. Work location, commute preferences, budget, property type and your household’s needs all matter. Use current local information and your own priorities when comparing neighborhoods.

A practical PCS purchase sequence

• Discuss eligibility, entitlement, budget and the expected move with the lender.

• Collect service, employment and other requested documents.

• Coordinate the home search and offer timeline with your real estate professional.

• Allow time for appraisal, any property conditions and underwriting.

• Confirm final closing arrangements before making firm moving commitments.

Property questions that deserve an early review

Ask about property condition, access, utilities and any well or septic systems before you rely on a particular financing plan. The VA appraisal and lender review may identify conditions to resolve. A separate home inspection helps you understand the property and is not replaced by the appraisal.

For installation housing and relocation contacts, use Military OneSource’s official installation directory. Check travel routes yourself and choose a location based on your household’s priorities.

Working with a local loan officer

Ask how your loan officer handles military timelines, communicates about documents and coordinates with the people involved in your purchase. Jerry is a lifelong Coloradan, and his approach centers on explaining options and keeping the next steps clear. Bring your expected reporting date and housing plans to the first conversation.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start before my orders are final?

You can begin a planning conversation and ask which documents are available now and which must follow. Your lender should review the effect of your expected move on qualification and occupancy.

Will I need a down payment?

Many eligible VA purchases require no down payment, but entitlement, lender approval, appraisal and transaction details affect the answer. Closing costs may still apply.

How long should I allow for closing?

Ask for a current estimate based on your transaction. Appraisal, property issues, documentation and underwriting can all affect timing.
