DSCR Loans in Colorado: The Complete 2026 Guide for Real Estate Investors

A DSCR loan may help a real estate investor qualify using a property’s rental income rather than personal tax returns as the main income measure. For Colorado investors, it is important to understand both the lender’s requirements and the local rules affecting the property’s intended rental use.

What DSCR means

DSCR stands for debt service coverage ratio. For many residential investment-loan programs, a lender compares qualifying rental income with the applicable housing payment. The exact income figure, payment components and documentation are determined by the lender’s program.

Meeting a ratio alone does not guarantee loan approval. Credit, down payment, reserves, property eligibility, ownership structure and other underwriting requirements may also apply.

How a lender may calculate the ratio

A common calculation divides qualifying monthly rent by principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues, often shortened to PITIA. The selected lender decides how to document rent and which costs to include.

Illustrative componentMonthly amount
Qualifying rental income$2,500
Principal and interest$1,550
Property taxes$250
Insurance$150
Association dues$50
Total PITIA$2,000
DSCR$2,500 ÷ $2,000 = 1.25

A ratio of 1.0 under a particular calculation means the counted income equals the counted payment. It does not establish that the property breaks even after all operating expenses, vacancy, maintenance and other costs. Evaluate investment cash flow separately.

The figures above are an arithmetic example, not a rate quote, actual transaction or promise of approval. They exclude vacancy, management, repairs and other operating expenses.

How DSCR financing compares with conventional financing

DSCR products may be useful when a borrower’s personal tax returns do not represent the basis the lender will use for rental-property qualification. Compare the available options using the same property and borrower scenario. Review rate, down payment, reserves, closing costs, prepayment terms and documentation requirements together.

Property types and ownership

Long-term rentals, short-term rentals and two-to-four-unit investment properties may be eligible. Whether a specific property or ownership structure qualifies depends on the chosen product. Ask about property condition, appraisal and rent documentation early in the process.

Short-term rentals in Colorado

Mountain communities can have different licensing, zoning, cap and operating requirements. Rules for an incorporated town may differ from those for surrounding unincorporated county areas. Confirm the jurisdiction and the property’s intended use before relying on projected short-term rental income.

A lender may apply different rules to short-term rental income than to a long-term lease or market-rent appraisal. Local permission to operate and lender acceptance of the income are separate questions.

Rates, costs and transaction review

DSCR pricing and terms vary by lender, borrower and property. Ratio, loan-to-value, credit, property type and other factors may influence the available offer. Obtain a scenario-specific review rather than treating a general article as a current rate quote.

Frequently asked questions

Can a DSCR loan be used for a Colorado short-term rental?

It may be possible under an eligible lender program and local rules. Both the property’s permitted use and the lender’s income treatment need review.

Does a strong ratio mean I am automatically approved?

No. The ratio is only part of the lender’s underwriting review.

Can I buy through an LLC?

Some products may allow an entity borrower, but the ownership and documentation requirements must be confirmed with Jerry’s lenders.
