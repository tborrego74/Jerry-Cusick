What a VA loan is and how it works in Colorado

A VA-backed home loan is a mortgage made by a private lender with a guaranty from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. That guaranty can support benefits such as no required down payment in many eligible purchase scenarios and no monthly mortgage insurance. The lender still reviews your ability to qualify and the property being financed.

VA-backed loans are different from the Native American Direct Loan program, in which VA is the lender for eligible borrowers and properties. Availability and requirements depend on the program.

Eligibility and the Certificate of Eligibility

Eligibility depends on service history and duty status. Active-duty service members generally meet the minimum service requirement after 90 continuous days; requirements for Veterans depend on when and how they served. Separate qualifying paths apply to National Guard and Reserve members, and certain surviving spouses may qualify. A Certificate of Eligibility, or COE, documents the borrower’s service-based eligibility and entitlement information.

The COE is one part of the process. It does not replace the lender’s credit, income, property and underwriting review.

Down payment and mortgage insurance

Many eligible VA purchases can be completed without a down payment, but the amount you can borrow and any cash needed depend on available entitlement, lender approval, the property’s appraised value and the transaction. No down payment does not mean there are no closing costs.

VA-backed loans do not charge monthly private mortgage insurance. A VA funding fee may apply unless the borrower qualifies for an exemption. Compare the full loan costs rather than focusing only on a single benefit.

The VA funding fee

The VA funding fee is a one-time program cost that may be financed or paid at closing. For a purchase loan with less than 5% down, the current fee is 2.15% for first use and 3.3% after first use. It falls to 1.5% with at least 5% down and 1.25% with at least 10% down. Certain borrowers are exempt, including qualifying recipients of service-connected disability compensation, qualifying surviving spouses receiving DIC and qualifying active-duty Purple Heart recipients.

Entitlement and county loan limits

Entitlement is the amount VA may guarantee to the lender. The $36,000 basic-entitlement figure shown on many COEs is not the borrower’s maximum loan amount. Borrowers with full entitlement do not have a VA-imposed loan limit, although lender qualification, purchase price and appraisal value still limit the transaction. When a borrower has reduced entitlement, remaining entitlement is calculated using the applicable county conforming loan limit and entitlement already charged.

Purchase, refinance and related VA benefits

VA home loan options include purchase loans, the Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL) for eligible existing VA loans, and cash-out refinancing. The Native American Direct Loan has separate eligibility and property requirements. Adapted-housing grants are grants, rather than an additional mortgage type. Energy-efficiency improvements may also be addressed through applicable program options.

The steps and timing from preparation to closing

There is no single guaranteed VA closing timeframe. Contract deadlines, borrower documentation, appraisal availability, property conditions and underwriting all affect the schedule. Jerry can provide a realistic target after reviewing the transaction.

• Discuss budget, documents and pre-approval with your lender.

• Choose a home and review any Colorado property-specific considerations.

• Complete the appraisal and required inspections; an appraisal is not a substitute for a home inspection.

• Resolve underwriting conditions, review closing figures and complete closing.

Colorado considerations

Military families around Colorado Springs may be coordinating a purchase with PCS orders. Rural and mountain properties can raise questions about access, utilities, wells, septic systems and condition. Some qualifying disabled veterans may also be eligible for a Colorado property tax benefit, subject to separate state requirements.

Questions to discuss with Jerry

Can I use my VA benefit again?

Possibly. Ask for an entitlement review, including any prior VA loan use and restoration requirements.

Can I start before I have my COE?

You can begin a discussion with a lender while the COE is being obtained. The lender will explain what is needed for the next stage.
